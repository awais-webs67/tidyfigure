Discount Calculator

To find a sale price, multiply the original price by (1 − discount ÷ 100). 25% off 120 is 120 × 0.75 = 90. Stacked discounts multiply rather than add: 25% off and then an extra 10% off is 120 × 0.75 × 0.9 = 81, a total of 32.5% off, not 35%.

How to calculate a discount

Amount saved = price × discount ÷ 100 Sale price = price × (1 − discount ÷ 100) 120 at 25% off: saved = 30, sale price = 90

Stacked discounts don't add up

Shops often advertise an extra discount on top of a sale: "25% off, plus an extra 10% at checkout". The extra 10% is taken from the already-reduced price, so the total is less than 35%.

Total discount = 1 − (1 − d₁) × (1 − d₂) 25% then 10% = 1 − 0.75 × 0.90 = 1 − 0.675 = 32.5% 50% then 20% = 1 − 0.50 × 0.80 = 60% (not 70%)

The order doesn't matter: 10% then 25% gives the same 32.5%.

Discounts and sales tax

In the United States, sales tax is added at the till, after discounts. In the UK, the EU and many other countries, shelf prices already include VAT, so leave the tax field at 0 unless your receipt adds it separately. When tax is added, it's calculated on the discounted price, so you pay less tax too.

Shopping smarter in sales

  • "Buy 2, get 1 free" is 33% off each item, but only if you'd buy three anyway.
  • "Up to 70% off" usually means only a few items reach 70%.
  • Check the original price. A discount from an inflated "was" price isn't a real saving. Price-history tools and receipts help.
  • Compare unit prices for groceries: a discounted large pack can still cost more per gram than a regular small one.

Working backwards

If you know the sale price and the discount, the original price is the sale price ÷ (1 − discount ÷ 100). A jacket that costs 63 after 30% off was 63 ÷ 0.7 = 90.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate 20% off a price?

Multiply the price by 0.8. For example, 20% off 75 is 75 × 0.8 = 60, a saving of 15.

Is 20% off plus an extra 10% the same as 30% off?

No. The extra 10% applies to the reduced price, so the total is 1 − 0.8 × 0.9 = 28% off.

How do I find the original price before a discount?

Divide the sale price by (1 − discount ÷ 100). If the sale price is 63 after 30% off, the original was 63 ÷ 0.7 = 90.

Is sales tax calculated before or after the discount?

Usually after. Tax is charged on the price you actually pay, which is the discounted price.

What does 50% off then 50% off equal?

75% off in total, not 100%: 1 − 0.5 × 0.5 = 0.75.
