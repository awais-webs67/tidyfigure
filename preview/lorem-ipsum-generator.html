What is lorem ipsum?

Lorem ipsum is scrambled Latin that printers and designers have used as dummy text since at least the 1500s. It comes from De finibus bonorum et malorum, a book on ethics written by Cicero in 45 BC, with words cut short and rearranged so it doesn't read as real sentences. "Lorem" itself is the end of the word "dolorem" (pain).

The point is that it looks like ordinary text, with a natural mix of short and long words, but it's meaningless, so people reviewing a design look at the layout and typography instead of reading the copy.

When to use placeholder text

  • Website and app mock-ups before the content is written
  • Testing layouts: how a card, column or button handles short and long text
  • Typography: comparing fonts, sizes and line spacing on realistic paragraphs
  • Templates for brochures, presentations and themes
  • Developers filling a page or database with sample content

When not to use it

Placeholder text can hide real problems. Actual headlines are often longer than the dummy ones, names come in every length, and translated text (German, for example) can be 30% longer than English. As soon as you know roughly what the content will say, test with realistic text. And always search for "lorem" before publishing: dummy text left on a live website is a common, embarrassing mistake.
