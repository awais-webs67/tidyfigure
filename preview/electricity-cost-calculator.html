Electricity Cost Calculator

Running cost = watts × hours ÷ 1,000 × price per kWh. A 2,000 W heater used 3 hours a day uses 6 kWh; at 0.25 per kWh that's 1.50 a day, about 45.60 a month. A 100 W TV on for 5 hours uses just 0.5 kWh a day, 0.13 at the same price.

Appliances

The power in watts (W) is on the label or in the manual. For appliances that cycle on and off (fridges, heaters with thermostats), use the average power, or the yearly kWh from the energy label ÷ 8,760 hours × 1,000.

The formula

kWh per day = watts × hours per day ÷ 1,000 Cost per day = kWh × price per kWh Heater: 2,000 W × 3 h = 6,000 Wh = 6 kWh × 0.25 = 1.50 a day × 30.4 days = 45.60 a month · × 365 = 547.50 a year

A kilowatt-hour (kWh) is the unit on your electricity bill: using 1,000 watts for one hour. Your price per kWh is on your bill or tariff, and it's in whatever currency you pay in, so the results are too.

Appliances that switch on and off

Fridges, freezers, water heaters and heaters with a thermostat don't run at full power all the time. A fridge rated at 150 W might average only 30–50 W. For these, it's more accurate to use the yearly energy from the energy label: divide the kWh per year by 8,760 (hours in a year) and multiply by 1,000 to get the average watts. For example, 300 kWh a year ÷ 8,760 × 1,000 = 34 W.

Standby power

Devices on standby (TVs, consoles, chargers, smart speakers) each use a little power all day. A games console using 10 W on standby 20 hours a day uses 73 kWh a year, about 18 at 0.25 per kWh. Switching off at the wall, or using smart plugs, removes this.

Ways to cut your bill

  • Focus on the appliances with the biggest share in the table: heating, cooling, drying and hot water are usually the largest.
  • Use time-of-use tariffs if available: run washing machines, dishwashers and EV charging at cheaper off-peak times.
  • Replace old bulbs with LEDs, which use about 80–90% less energy than incandescent bulbs.
  • Air-dry clothes when you can, and run full loads in washing machines and dishwashers.
  • For heating, a heat pump or air conditioner in heating mode can deliver 3–4 kWh of heat for every 1 kWh of electricity.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate the cost of running an appliance?

Multiply the watts by the hours used, divide by 1,000 to get kWh, then multiply by your price per kWh.

How much does it cost to run a 2000W heater?

2 kWh per hour. At 0.25 per kWh, that is 0.50 an hour, or 1.50 for three hours a day.

How many kWh does a fridge use?

A modern fridge-freezer typically uses 200–400 kWh a year, which averages about 25–45 W.

What is a kWh?

A kilowatt-hour: the energy used by a 1,000-watt appliance running for one hour. It is the unit on electricity bills.

Where do I find my electricity price?

On your bill or your supplier’s tariff page, shown as a price per kWh (sometimes per unit).
