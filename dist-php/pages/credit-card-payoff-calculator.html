How credit card interest works

Card interest is quoted as an APR, but charged monthly on the balance you carry: roughly APR ÷ 12 each month. At 24% APR, that's 2% a month, so a 3,500 balance costs about 70 in interest in the first month. Only the part of your payment above the interest reduces the debt.

Monthly interest ≈ balance × APR ÷ 12 Balance next month = balance + interest − payment

The minimum payment trap

Minimum payments are designed to keep you in debt for a long time: often just 1–3% of the balance plus interest. As the balance falls, the minimum falls too, so it can take 10–20 years to clear a card and the interest can exceed the original spending. Paying a fixed amount above the minimum, and keeping it the same as the balance falls, is the single most effective change.

Strategies for paying off cards faster

  • Avalanche: pay the minimum on every card and put any extra money on the card with the highest interest rate. This saves the most interest.
  • Snowball: put extra money on the smallest balance first. Quick wins keep many people motivated.
  • Balance transfer: moving debt to a 0% card can save a lot, but check the transfer fee (often 2–4%) and have a plan to clear it before the offer ends.
  • Stop adding to it: use a debit card while you pay down the balance.
  • Talk to your lender if you're struggling. Free debt advice services can help negotiate lower payments.

Why paying more saves so much

Because interest is charged on the remaining balance, every extra payment reduces all future interest. In the example above, raising the payment from 150 to 225 a month cuts the time to about 1 year 7 months and saves roughly 530 in interest.

Estimates assume a fixed APR and no new spending or fees. Your card statement shows your exact figures.

Frequently asked questions

How long will it take to pay off my credit card?

Enter your balance, APR and monthly payment. The calculator shows the months to pay off and the interest you’ll pay.

How is credit card interest calculated?

Roughly APR ÷ 12 is charged each month on the balance you carry.

Why isn’t my balance going down?

If your payment barely covers the monthly interest, the balance hardly falls. Increase your payment above the interest amount.

Is it better to pay more than the minimum?

Yes. Paying a fixed amount above the minimum can save years and thousands in interest.

Which card should I pay off first?

The highest-interest card saves the most money (avalanche); the smallest balance first can be more motivating (snowball).
