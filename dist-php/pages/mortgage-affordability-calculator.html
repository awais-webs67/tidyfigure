Mortgage Affordability Calculator

Lenders commonly use the 28/36 rule: housing costs up to 28% of gross monthly income, and all debt payments up to 36%. On a 90,000 income with 400 a month of other debts and 60,000 down, at 6.5% over 30 years, that allows about 2,100 a month for housing, or a home of about 327,500.

How lenders decide what you can afford

Lenders look at your debt-to-income ratio (DTI): the share of your gross (before-tax) monthly income that goes on debt payments. There are two limits:

  • Front-end (housing) ratio: mortgage payment, property tax, insurance and any HOA fee, divided by gross monthly income. The classic limit is 28%.
  • Back-end (total) ratio: housing costs plus all other debt payments (car loan, student loans, credit card minimums), divided by gross income. The classic limit is 36%.

Your maximum housing cost is whichever limit is lower. Some loan programmes accept more, such as 31/43, which is the "flexible" option above.

Worked example

Income 90,000 a year = 7,500 a month Housing limit: 7,500 × 28% = 2,100 Total limit: 7,500 × 36% = 2,700 − 400 other debts = 2,300 Max housing cost = lower of the two = 2,100 a month At 6.5% over 30 years, with tax + insurance at 1.5% of the price a year: Home price ≈ 327,479 (60,000 down + 267,479 loan) Mortgage payment 1,690.65 + tax & insurance 409.35 = 2,100.00

Interest rates have a large effect: here, each percentage point changes the price you can afford by about 7–8%. Paying off debts helps when the total-debt limit is the one holding you back. The note under the result tells you which limit applies.

Affordable for the bank, or for you?

These ratios are what lenders allow, not necessarily what's comfortable. Before you buy, check the payment against your real budget (the budget planner helps), and remember the costs that aren't in the ratio:

  • Closing costs, legal fees and stamp duty or transfer taxes, often 2–5% of the price.
  • Maintenance: a common guide is 1% of the home's value per year.
  • Utilities, furniture and moving costs.
  • An emergency fund of 3–6 months' expenses that you don't spend on the deposit.

Down payment and mortgage insurance

A bigger deposit lowers the loan and the monthly payment. In many countries, a deposit under 20% means the lender adds mortgage insurance (PMI in the US) or charges a higher rate. Rules differ by country and lender, so treat this calculator's figure as a starting point for a conversation with a lender or broker.

This is an estimate. Lenders also look at your credit history, job stability and the property itself.

Frequently asked questions

How much house can I afford on my salary?

Under the 28/36 rule, your housing costs can be up to 28% of your gross monthly income. Enter your income, debts, deposit and rate above to turn that into a home price.

What is the 28/36 rule?

Housing costs should be at most 28% of gross monthly income, and all debt payments together at most 36%.

Does the calculator include property tax and insurance?

Yes. Enter them as a yearly percentage of the price (1–2% is common). Add any HOA or service charge separately.

How does the interest rate affect affordability?

A higher rate means more of each payment is interest, so the same payment supports a smaller loan. One percentage point often changes the affordable price by about 7–8%.

Should I borrow the maximum I can afford?

Not necessarily. Leave room for maintenance, savings and rate rises. Check the payment against your real monthly budget.
